# Email to Cheryl

*For review. Nothing has been sent.*

**To:** Cheryl
**Subject:** The forest app letter - where it got to, and why I have probably stopped

---

Hi Cheryl,

Thank you for taking a look at that Forestry England letter. I owe you an update, because the version
you have is now three rewrites out of date and, more to the point, I have talked myself out of
sending it at all. Here is how that happened, because the process turned out to be more interesting
than the letter.

**What I was originally asking for.** Two things: would they buy the app off me, and if not would
they let me put it on the app stores. That was the version you saw.

**Then I actually read the licences.** I had been assuming I was on shaky ground, because I take the
forest details off their website rather than from a nice tidy feed. It turns out I am not:

- Their own copyright page says the information on their website can be reused free of charge under
  the Open Government Licence, as long as I do not use their logos or photographs. I do not.
- The car park data is published under the same licence, and its official record says in as many
  words that there are no access constraints on it.
- That licence explicitly allows commercial use. So selling it, or taking donations, needs nobody's
  permission.
- There is no terms of use page on their website at all, and their robots file does not exclude the
  pages I read. So there is nothing I am in breach of.

So the whole premise of the letter, "please may I", was wrong. **I already may.**

**The one thing that is not covered is their name.** Trade marks are specifically excluded from that
licence. So I cannot call it "the Forestry England app", but I can perfectly well say it finds
forests managed by Forestry England, which is simply a true statement about what it does. That is a
naming decision, not a permission problem.

**Then I had it torn apart.** I got three people to read the latest draft cold, knowing nothing about
it, and asked them to find the holes. They were brutal and they agreed with each other, which is the
bit that convinced me:

- It was far too long. 700 words to a general enquiries inbox is a proposal, not an enquiry, and
  proposals are somebody else's problem to deal with.
- It asked for about ten things at once. Nobody in that inbox could say yes to all of it, so it would
  get forwarded twice and then quietly die.
- The line I was proudest of, where I said I would not use their name without their agreement, was
  the worst thing in it. I had handed them the outcome they would have picked anyway, for free, in
  writing. Replying costs them time and creates a record. Saying nothing costs them nothing and gets
  them exactly what they want. I had removed every reason to answer me.
- And asking and being told no would leave me worse off than never asking, because then I would
  actually have been refused.

**Where I have landed.** Two separate things that I had been jamming into one letter:

1. **The app does not need them.** I will publish it, keep it free, name it something of my own, say
   plainly what it does, credit them properly, and put a small "buy me a coffee" link in it for
   anyone who finds it useful. None of that requires a conversation with anybody.
2. **What I actually want is work.** Ideally to be brought in to improve their website and build
   things properly. That is a completely different approach, to a named person rather than a general
   inbox, and it is much stronger *after* I have shipped something than before. "Here is a working
   app people use" beats "here is an idea, may I".

So the letter is parked rather than binned. I would rather approach them holding something finished.

**Two drafts attached**, because you might find the comparison useful and because you spent time on
the first one:

- **Draft A** is the lean one, about 550 words. Problem, solution, ask. I think it is the better
  piece of writing.
- **Draft B** is the fuller one, about 780 words, with everything I wanted to say in it. It is also
  the one the reviewers took apart, which rather makes the point about waffle.

You will notice A is better and shorter, which I suspect will not surprise you.

Anyway, thank you for reading the original. It genuinely helped, not least because "keep the waffle
to a minimum" turned out to be the note that everything else eventually agreed with.

Many Thanks,

Rob
